Tina's Take on NeilMed SINUS RINSE™ Bottle

Journey Mapping · Hierarchical Task Analysis (HTA) · Miro

From: Personal Project 🧚

What this is: This project involved creating a journey map for the NeilMed product, visualizing the user's experience from initial contact to post-purchase support.

Why I wanted to do this: As a long-term NeilMed SINUS RINSE™ user myself, I have always thought the procedure of carrying out a rinse with the device was always cognitively taxxing - which diverted me away from using the product consistently even when I most needed it! Therefore, I decided that it would be insightful to map out the whole user journey process as a user myself and as somebody who studies journey mapping through a systems design approach. I put up real photos of myself during each stage to more accurately capture the emotions and feelings and painpoints etc. more realistically - hoping it provides a better depiction of the user's experience throughout the stages.

Section I: Journey Mapping

The journey map illustrates the user's interactions with the NeilMed product, highlighting key touchpoints, pain points, and opportunities for improvement. It provides a comprehensive overview of the user's experience, from initial awareness to post-purchase support.

Section II: Hierarchical Task Analysis (HTA)

I conducted a hierarchical task analysis for the NeilMed product, identifying and documenting the tasks required and their respective difficulty levels for effective use.

Task Difficulty
1. Read Instructions. This requires pinpointing the location of the instructions. The instructions are visible but contain substantial text that drain out text specific to the purchased product - The instruction manual contains instructions for other nasal irrigation devices (SINUS RINSE™, SINOFLO ® READY RINSE®, SinuGator® Cordless Pulsating Nasal Wash, NASAFLO® NETI POT, NasaMist® All-in-One NasaDrops®, Naspira Drops®, NasoGel® Spray and NasoGel® Tube, Isotonic, Hypertonic & Pediatric NasaMist®, Clearcanal®, etc.). This causes unnecessary confusion, page flipping and difficulty locating instructions for the purchased product. Moderate
2. Finding the right time to use (eg, at least 1hr before bedtime). Must plan ahead before use due to nasal drainage. It is instructionally advised to use the rinse bottle at least 1 hour before bedtime or lying down. However, this advice has been observed to be inconsistent where nasal drainage remains an issue for the user several hours after use. Moderate
3. Water preparation (water volume, water temperature, water sterilization). The process to prepare sterilized water is tedious in a way where the manual specifically asks for either of the following: Rinsing bottle with non-sterilized water RO water, boiled then cooled down water, bottled spring water, distilled water, or micro-filtered (through 0.2micron filter) water. This requires a lot of thinking on the user, as the user is also given the opportunity to choose the best option based on the availability of their resources for a rinse at a particular time of day. Difficult
4. Rinse bottle with prepared water. This is a step that can be easily forgotten or done subconsciously with non-sterilized water instead. Rinsing the bottle with non-sterilized water is considered a sanitary risk and may open up more room for infection or other side effects upon entering the sinus system. Moderate
5. Fill bottle with water to line. This requires eye-level measurement and can easily be overestimated. You may end up wasting some of the water that was prepared in earlier steps and not leave enough of it for cleaning the bottle after use. Moderate
6. Add in contents of saline packet. Opening saline packet requires low strength with fingers only; no scissors needed. Easy
7. Shake bottle with nose cap on. This is a step that is easily forgotten, but it is an easy task to do. However, it requires the user to remember to keep the nose cap sealed while shaking up the bottle to properly dissolve the saline packet contents in the water without water spilling out. Easy
8. Tissue preparation for in-between rinses. This is a recommended task as after every rinse through one nostril, you will immediately experience volumes of fluid discharge consisting of the NeilMed solution and mucus relieved from your sinus system. However, the acquisition of these tissues is a simple task, both physically and cognitively. Easy
9. Nasal irrigation step. This is where users spray the saline solution into their sinuses with the squeeze bottle. Head and body positioning will have to be held at a calculated angle that allows for proper nasal irrigation. Taking into consideration gravitational forces and the direction in which the head tilts, this step requires physical agility for an appropriate bottle orientation – from hip hinging to head tilts to elbow angle at which the bottle is being held from. The squeeze force required to administer the nasal irrigation step with the bottle also requires more than the recommended amount according to standard design principles once over half of the water in the bottle is used up. The squeeze force also must be well-controlled within a force bracket as too much force for an easily squeezable amount of water could bring too much pressure onto the user's sinuses. Improper use of bottle during nasal irrigation could lead to the most side effects, such as long-term nasal drainage, choking fears, fluid going down the wrong passageways, and physical injury from uncomfortable head tilts, body positioning, or squeeze force on fingers during use. Difficult
10. Clean device with prepared water. Many users underestimate cleaning importance and assume the use of tap water or non-sterilized water is fine for cleaning. This is careless preparation for the next time the same device is used. Difficult
11. Dry device accordingly. Many users underestimate drying importance and the sanitation involved in the materials used to dry nasal irrigation devices. Paper towels must be clean, and devices must be dried in a non-humid environment to prevent bacterial growth. Moderate
