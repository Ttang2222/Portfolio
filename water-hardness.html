A systems-design project exploring how connected hardware sensors and an interactive dashboard can help building management teams monitor water-softening performance and identify potential maintenance issues.

01 / Problem Framing

Understanding the Problem

Water hardness is a common issue in building management, affecting both the efficiency of water systems and the longevity of appliances. Hard water contains high levels of calcium and magnesium ions, which can lead to scale buildup in pipes and fixtures, reducing water flow and increasing maintenance costs. Understanding and managing water hardness is crucial for ensuring optimal performance and sustainability in building operations.

02 / Stakeholder Research

Stakeholders & Mapping the Process

Initially, our team approached the problem through the lens of primary stakeholders: the immediate water users. However, we recognized that this approach could make the solution less usable or accessible for everyday users. We reconsidered our stakeholder focus before the user-testing phase.

04 / Design Decision

Narrowing Down the Solution Approach

Following our brainstorming and storyboarding phases, our team shifted away from a region-centric model to adopt a targeted, building-by-building approach. This strategy allowed us to tailor solutions to the specific infrastructure and needs of each facility, making the system more feasible to design, implement, and manage within our course timeline.

By focusing on individual sites, we aimed to empower building managers with intuitive tools to monitor water hardness, improve water-system management, and reduce long-term maintenance costs. To support prototyping and user testing within scope, we developed an interactive online dashboard tailored specifically for building managers.

Key design decision: Focus the monitoring system on individual buildings and provide building managers with a digital dashboard for monitoring and scaling the solution.

05 / Proposed Solution

Final Solution

The final solution combines three real-time hardware sensors (brine level, conductivity, and flow rate) with an Internet of Things (IoT) infrastructure and the SmartSoft prototype app. Building management and facility teams can use the dashboard to view water-system measurements and identify potential maintenance needs.

App Prototype: SmartSoft

05.1 / Hardware Architecture

Understanding the Hardware Sensors

The SmartSoft system incorporates a brine-level sensor, a conductivity sensor, and a flow-rate sensor. These sensors are positioned within the water-softening system to provide information about water quality and system operation. The brine-level sensor monitors the salt/brine supply, the conductivity sensor measures electrical conductivity as an indicator of water quality, and the flow-rate sensor tracks the volume of water passing through the system.

Sensor 01

Flow Rate Sensor

The flow-rate sensor measures the rate of water entering the water softener, providing information about how much water passes through the system over time. It monitors the volumetric flow of incoming hard, unsoftened water from a ground or surface water source. In the proposed system, flow data can help monitor operational capacity and validate other measurements.

Two Sensing Approaches Considered

Option A

Turbine / Impeller Sensor

An inline turbine rotates as water flows through the pipe. A magnet embedded in the turbine is detected by a Hall-effect sensor, producing electrical pulses as the turbine rotates. By counting these pulses, the system can estimate the water flow rate, typically reported in L/min or m³/h.

Option B

Ultrasonic Time-of-Flight Sensor

An ultrasonic sensor estimates flow by detecting changes in the travel time of ultrasonic signals through moving water. Unlike a turbine-based sensor, it can be mounted externally on the pipe using a clamp.

Placement consideration: The turbine sensor is installed inline with the water supply, whereas the ultrasonic sensor can be externally clamped onto the pipe. This difference was an important consideration when evaluating potential sensor configurations.

Sensor 02

Brine-Level Sensor

The brine-level sensor monitors the salt and brine supply inside the softener’s brine tank to help ensure that regeneration can occur properly. If the brine level becomes too low, the system can alert building management before the softener begins producing hard water.

How It Works

A float is positioned vertically inside the brine tank and moves up and down with the brine level. A magnetic reed switch detects the float's position and produces a simple digital signal (0/1). When the float drops below a defined level, the signal is sent to the IoT controller, which can trigger a low-level alert on the building management dashboard.

Sensor Options Considered

  • Float sensor: Simple, reliable, and relatively low cost; suitable for detecting whether sufficient brine is available.
  • Ultrasonic level sensor: Provides a more precise measurement of liquid level without physical contact.
  • Guided Wave Radar: Offers accurate level measurement but is more complex and costly.
  • Electronic salt-level sensors: Can provide more detailed information about remaining salt, such as an estimated percentage.

Design Consideration

For a condo or apartment building application, the float sensor was considered a practical option because the primary requirement was to determine whether there was enough salt/brine for reliable regeneration, rather than continuously measuring the exact liquid level. The design therefore prioritizes reliability and cost-effectiveness while providing an actionable low-level warning before softener failure occurs.

Sensor 03

Conductivity Sensor

The conductivity sensor monitors water quality at the outlet of the water softener, providing an indication of when the softening process may no longer be effectively removing hardness from the water.

How It Works

A conductivity probe is positioned in a small sampling chamber connected to the softener's outlet pipe. The probe uses two electrodes and applies a small AC current between them to measure how readily electricity passes through the water.

Water hardness is primarily associated with dissolved calcium (Ca²⁺) and magnesium (Mg²⁺) ions. As the concentration of dissolved ions increases, electrical conductivity generally increases as well. Therefore, a rise in conductivity can be used as an indicator of changing water quality and potential hard-water breakthrough.

What a Rising Reading May Indicate

  • Resin exhaustion: The softener's ion-exchange resin may be reaching its capacity.
  • Increasing hardness: More hardness ions may be passing through the softener.
  • Hard-water breakthrough: Untreated or insufficiently softened water may be entering the building's water system.

Role in the monitoring system: The conductivity sensor can provide an early warning of declining softener performance. Combined with flow-rate and brine-level measurements, it can help identify potential problems and provide building management with actionable information.

System Integration

IoT Controller

The IoT controller acts as the central integration point between the water softener's physical sensors and the remote monitoring dashboard. It combines a microcontroller, sensor interfaces, and communication capabilities to collect, process, and transmit information from the softener.

How It Works

The microcontroller receives signals from each sensor and converts them into usable data:

Digital Signal (0/1)

Brine-level sensor.

Analog Signal

Conductivity sensor.

Pulse / Frequency Signal

Flow-rate sensor.

The controller can then apply basic logic to identify conditions such as low brine levels, increasing conductivity, or abnormal flow rates.

Connecting the System

Beyond local processing, the IoT controller provides the communication layer needed to transmit sensor data to the cloud and ultimately the building management dashboard. Depending on the system architecture, this communication could use technologies such as Wi-Fi, LoRa, Zigbee, or Ethernet.

This makes the IoT controller the bridge between the physical water-softening system and the digital monitoring interface, enabling remote monitoring, data visualization, and automated alerts.

Sensors → IoT Controller → Cloud → Monitoring Dashboard

The controller therefore transforms individual sensor measurements into actionable information that building management can use to monitor softener performance and respond to potential issues.

06 / Evaluation

User Testing

User outreach was conducted through email, in-person office visits, and phone calls. The SmartSoft prototype link was provided alongside a Google Form for feedback. Participants were building managers and facility maintenance personnel from five different building management organizations. Each participant was asked to complete a series of tasks using the SmartSoft app prototype, and their feedback was recorded for analysis.

Evaluation focus: Participants interacted with the dashboard prototype and provided feedback to support assessment of the proposed interface.
